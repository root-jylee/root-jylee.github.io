About

ROOT Lab studies physical-layer security, privacy, and trust by transforming physical signals into roots of trust for intelligent secure systems. Rather than being limited to a particular communication medium, our research focuses on the common physical-layer principles that govern communication systems.

Our central question is: what can an adversary infer from observable physical signals, and how can we limit, verify, or control that inference? We approach this question through signal processing, information theory, statistical inference, privacy theory, and intelligent system design.

Adversarial Inference and Security Methods

ROOT Lab studies security methods according to the stage of adversarial inference, where each stage corresponds to a different question that an adversary may ask from observable physical signals.

  • Transmission inference: covert communication protects the existence of communication by hiding whether a transmission has occurred.
  • Identity inference: anonymity and authentication protect or verify the entity that generated a physical signal.
  • Content inference: physical-layer secrecy protects transmitted information by preventing an adversary from recovering the message, signal, or learning update.
  • Data-contribution inference: differential privacy limits whether an adversary can infer that an individual data record affected a communication-efficient learning update.

Research Interests

  • Physical-layer authentication, RF fingerprinting, and root-of-trust mechanisms
  • Covert and low-probability-of-detection communication
  • Physical-layer secrecy, artificial noise, and key generation
  • Physical-layer privacy for communication-efficient federated learning
  • Trustworthy physical-layer design for intelligent secure systems
