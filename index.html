About

ROOT Lab studies physical-layer security, privacy, and trust by transforming physical signals into roots of trust for intelligent secure systems. Rather than being limited to a particular communication medium, our research focuses on the common physical-layer principles behind communication and distributed intelligence systems.

Our central question is: what can an adversary infer from observable physical signals, and how can we limit, verify, or control that inference? We approach this question through signal processing, information theory, statistical inference, privacy theory, and intelligent system design.

Security and Privacy Layers

ROOT Lab views physical-layer security as a set of defenses against progressively deeper adversarial inference.

  • Covert communication: hiding whether a transmission exists.
  • Anonymity and authentication: protecting or verifying who generated a physical signal.
  • Content confidentiality: preventing an adversary from recovering what was transmitted.
  • Differential privacy: limiting whether individual data records contributed to a released learning update.

Research Interests

  • Physical-layer authentication, RF fingerprinting, and root-of-trust mechanisms
  • Covert and low-probability-of-detection communication
  • Physical-layer secrecy, artificial noise, and key generation
  • Physical-layer privacy for distributed AI and federated learning
  • Secure ISAC and trustworthy cyber-physical communication systems
