Research

From physical and statistical structure to secure communication-system design

Research Philosophy & Overview · 연구 철학 및 개요

ROOT Lab studies how the physical and statistical structure of communication systems creates distinguishability, randomness, asymmetry, and uncertainty — and how those properties can be exploited or controlled to establish security. We then connect them to system parameters, resource allocation, protocols, and communication-system design.

The four axes are the security problems we study. Fingerprinting, challenge–response, beamforming, cooperation, optimization, machine learning, OTA/AirComp, and related techniques are methods used across these problems, not separate research axes.

Long-term extension. ROOT Lab currently centers on classical communication systems. Quantum communication security is a future extension, with PLKG → QKD as a natural starting point.

Physical-Layer Security Resources · 물리계층 보안 자원

ROOT Lab treats the physical layer not as a simple transmission pipe, but as a source of physical evidence and statistical structure that can support different security functions.

Channel Reciprocity / Randomness

Fading · multipath · delay → shared randomness for PLKG.

Device-Specific Impairments

CFO · I/Q imbalance · PA nonlinearity · phase noise → PLA and fingerprinting.

Geometry / Mobility Consistency

Doppler · RTT · AoA · trajectory · orbit → behavior and identity consistency for PLA.

Noise / Interference / Uncertainty

Background noise and interference structure → reduced detectability for covert communication.

Spatial Heterogeneity

User distribution and network geometry → statistical indistinguishability for covert communication.

Channel Advantage / Propagation Asymmetry

Main-channel and wiretap-channel differences → potential secrecy advantage.

Channel Noise / Signal Superposition

Receiver noise and aggregate waveforms → reduced individual inference for PHY-aided DP.

System-Oriented Security Design · 시스템 중심 설계

Our work connects physical-layer security resources to communication-system design and optimization. We ask not only what physical phenomenon exists, but also how system parameters, resources, protocols, and algorithms should be designed around it.

Distributed AI Systems

Federated learning · split inference · OTA/AirComp · swarm intelligence. Examples include covert gradient transmission, physical authentication of participating nodes, PLKG-assisted secure aggregation, and PHY-aided DP.

Physical AI Systems

Autonomous vehicles · UAVs · robots · autonomous ships · smart factories. Physical fingerprints and behavior-consistency evidence can support authentication and forged-command detection.

Practical Communication Systems

3GPP LTE/5G/6G · Wi-Fi · LoRa · fiber and optical interconnects. We are interested in bringing physical-layer security into practical protocols and operating environments.

Emerging system paradigms. ISAC → ISCC · Cell-Free / O-RAN · UAV / NTN / LEO Satellite. These are application or system paradigms, not separate core research axes.

Communication Systems & Media · 통신 매체 확장성

The boundary of ROOT Lab is the communication system, not a single propagation medium. RF / wireless is the current center, with broader extensions where physical phenomena can provide useful security resources.

Research Framework · 연구 수행의 4계층

01

Foundational Theory

Information theory · detection theory · estimation theory · probability · linear algebra — identify what is fundamentally possible.

02

Algorithm & Intelligence

Optimization · ML / DL / RL · Agentic AI — design security strategies under resource constraints.

03

System Implementation

Digital communication systems · DSP · PHY / baseband · SDR — implement theory and algorithms as real signals and systems.

04

Physical & Application Domain

RF · acoustic · optical · wired / distributed AI · physical AI · practical communications — provide the physical phenomena and mission environment.

Long-term direction. The same ROOT philosophy can be extended beyond classical communication systems toward quantum communication security when that becomes an active research axis.
