Research

Four adversarial questions, translated into physical-layer trust architectures.

Research scope

What can an adversary infer?

ROOT Lab develops security, privacy, and trust methods around observable physical signals. Each research scope begins with a specific adversarial inference goal, uses a physical-layer resource, and ends with a measurable trust outcome.

Choose a scope below to jump to its system architecture.

01Covert communication 02Physical-layer authentication 03Physical-layer secrecy 04Differential privacy
Shared framework

Method → physical layer → system.

The four scopes share a common path: define what must remain hidden or verifiable, exploit physical observables, then validate trust in a real communication system.

02 · Identity

Adversary asks
“Who generated the signal?”
Physical resources
Spatial channels, hardware traits, mobility, 3D channel maps
Trust outcome
Robust identity decision from a packet or challenge–response exchange
System architecture

→

04 · Privacy

Differential Privacy

Protect each participant’s data contribution while still enabling useful distributed learning.

Adversary asks
“Was individual data exposed?”
Physical resources
Wireless superposition, aggregation, channel noise, power control
Trust outcome
Useful global learning under an explicit privacy budget
System architecture
01 · InputsLocal data + model updatesDistributed user contributions
→
02 · Privacy mechanismClip + perturb + transmitCalibrated noise and power
→
03 · OTA aggregationWireless superpositionAggregate without raw updates
→

Threat modelAn observer or aggregator attempts to infer whether a participant or record influenced the learned model.
Application systems

Where the architectures operate.

Each architecture is adapted to the observables and constraints of its deployment environment.

Terrestrial networks

Secure NOMA, artificial-noise-aided transmission, and multi-user covert communication.

NTN & autonomous platforms

UAVs, USVs, satellites, maritime networks, and mobile cyber-physical systems.

Private distributed learning

Wireless federated learning and over-the-air aggregation with privacy guarantees.
