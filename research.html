Research

Trust rooted in
the physical layer.

We connect security methods with physical signals, channels, mobility, hardware characteristics, interference, and noise.

Research scope

What can an adversary infer?

ROOT Lab develops physical-layer security, privacy, and trust methods for communication systems. Our work connects covert communication, physical-layer authentication, physical-layer secrecy, and differential privacy with the physical-layer communication domain.

Our central question is what an adversary can infer from observable physical signals—and how we can limit, verify, or control that inference.

Security & privacy methods

Four questions shape our work.

01

Covert communication

Hide the existence of transmissions from a warden while maintaining reliable communication.

Did transmission occur?
02

Physical-layer authentication

Verify a transmitter through spatial channels, hardware traits, and physical consistency.

Who generated the signal?
03

Physical-layer secrecy

Use information-theoretic security and artificial noise to protect transmitted content.

What was transmitted?
04

Differential privacy

Protect individual data contributions in wireless learning and over-the-air aggregation.

Was individual data exposed?
Application systems

From channels to systems.

Trust is established from signals, channels, and devices—not from software-layer assumptions alone.

Terrestrial base stations

Secure NOMA, artificial-noise-aided transmission, and multi-user covert communication.

NTN & autonomous platforms

UAVs, USVs, satellites, maritime networks, and mobile cyber-physical systems.

Private distributed learning

Privacy and security for wireless federated learning and over-the-air aggregation.

Representative themes

Research directions

01

Covert communication in multi-user networks

Cooperative users, spatial heterogeneity, and channel correlation conceal transmissions while maintaining reliability.

02

Physical-layer authentication for mobile platforms

Challenge-response mechanisms use spatial channels, 3D channel maps, and mobility-induced consistency.

03

Physical-layer secrecy and private learning

Information-theoretic security, artificial noise, differential privacy, and over-the-air aggregation protect data.

04

Awareness-driven intelligent security

Adaptive systems sense, localize, and reason about adversaries for closed-loop protection.
