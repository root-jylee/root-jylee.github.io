Research

Finding security resources in physical signals and communication environments

Research Philosophy & Overview

Every communication signal is affected by the channel, noise, hardware, location, and motion around it. These effects leave physical clues in the received signal. ROOT Lab studies how those clues can be used to make communication harder to detect, easier to authenticate, safer for key generation, and more private.

Our four research areas ask four simple questions: Can communication be hidden? Can a transmitter be trusted? Can two devices create a shared secret? Can private data remain difficult to infer?

Long-term direction. ROOT Lab currently focuses on classical communication systems while keeping the same research philosophy open to future quantum communication security.
01 · Existence

Covert Communication

Question
Can communication take place without an observer easily noticing that it happened?
Physical clue
Noise, interference, and uncertainty can make a weak transmission harder to distinguish from the background.
Goal
Communicate reliably while keeping the transmission difficult to detect.
Basic idea
01 · SignalA legitimate transmitter sends dataThe signal travels through a noisy communication environment.
→
02 · ObservationAn outside observer sees an uncertain signalThe observer tries to decide whether communication occurred.
→
03 · OutcomeReliable but hard-to-detect communicationUseful for systems where hiding the existence of transmission matters.
In one sentenceHide the existence of communication.
02 · Authenticity

Physical-Layer Authentication

Question
Is this signal really coming from the device or transmitter it claims to come from?
Physical clue
Hardware, channel, location, speed, and direction can leave distinctive traces on a received signal.
Goal
Use those traces as physical evidence to distinguish legitimate signals from forged ones.
Basic idea
01 · ReceiveObserve the incoming signalThe receiver measures physical features of the signal.
→
02 · CompareCheck whether the physical evidence is consistentFor example, hardware or motion-related characteristics.
→
03 · DecideAccept or reject the transmitterA forged signal should look physically inconsistent.
In one sentenceUse physical evidence to verify who sent the signal.
03 · Secret Key

Physical-Layer Key Generation

Question
Can two communicating devices create the same secret without sending the secret itself?
Physical clue
Two devices connected by the same wireless channel can observe similar random variations that another device sees differently.
Goal
Turn shared channel randomness into a common secret key.
Basic idea
01 · ObserveTwo devices measure the same channelBoth sides see related random variations.
→
02 · ExtractConvert the shared randomness into bitsThe two devices process their measurements.
→
03 · ShareObtain the same secret keyA different observer should have difficulty reproducing it.
In one sentenceTurn shared physical randomness into a secret key.
04 · Data Privacy

Physical-Layer-Aided Differential Privacy

Question
Can a communication system learn from many users without revealing too much about any one user?
Physical clue
Wireless noise and the natural mixing of transmitted signals can help hide the contribution of an individual user.
Goal
Use communication-layer effects together with privacy techniques to reduce individual-data inference.
Basic idea
01 · UsersMany devices contribute data or model updatesEach contribution contains information about one participant.
→
02 · Wireless mixingSignals combine with noise in the channelThe physical layer can become part of the privacy mechanism.
→
03 · OutcomeUseful learning with less individual exposureProtect privacy while preserving useful system performance.
In one sentenceUse the physical layer to help protect individual data.
